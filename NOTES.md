## 2026-09-16 — 1969 Dodge Charger Daytona
- Engine: road curvature. `bend(y)` shifts a row sideways on one sine wave — 13 px of swing at `curve: 1`, wavelength 760 px, new optional `game.curve` (0–1, default 0.6, 0 = dead straight). The shift is a function of `y - scroll` rather than screen y, so a road feature keeps its own offset as it scrolls past and the car and an obstacle at the same `y` are always displaced by the same amount. It is a draw-time effect only: `update()` never calls it, so collision, lane centres and near misses stay in unbent lane space and are byte-for-byte unchanged. Road surface and edge lines are painted row by row when the curve is on and as three tall rects when it is off, so a straight day costs nothing. `prefers-reduced-motion` forces the amplitude to 0 — the lateral drift is the whole effect and the road already scrolls without it. `scripts/check.mjs` validates `game.curve`; `docs/GAME.md` documents it.

## 2026-09-17 — 1966 Mini Cooper S (works rally)
- Engine: weather per scenery. A `WEATHER` table of three particle kinds (`rain`, `snow`, `dust`) plus `SCENERY_WEATHER` mapping `coast` → rain, `snow` → snow, `desert` → dust; every other scenery stays dry. New optional `game.weather` overrides it: `auto` (default), `none`, or a kind by name, so a dry coast or a snowy mountain pass is possible. Each particle has its own fall speed and a `tow` share of the current road speed — rain (tow 0.85) leans hard into a fast run, snow (0.12) hangs almost still while the road rushes past, dust (0.45) blows sideways on a 44 px/s drift. `stepWeather()` runs from `frame()` rather than `update()`, so the idle and crash cards sit in weather too; `update()` never touches it, so nothing here is collidable and the lane/near-miss maths is untouched.
